## Mechanics of this world (locked rules)
- **The Engine** — an ancient, powerful device (the halo ring). It "summons" the **Soul Stars** of great dead people, tuned to graves. It is nearly out of power and needs a long recharge. It "pulls in" what it recognizes.
- **Soul Star** — a compact mass of a great dead person's soul; it travels through the void as a small white star; when it touches a living soul it merges, causing soul-level agony. The Star *chooses* its host; forced hosts (Vex's 9 extractions) rot within a year. A settled Star carries "a small gift of the life it carried" — Sean's gift so far: hearing the dead language as his mother-tongue. More gifts may surface.
- **Extraction** — tearing a Star out of a host. Nine successes, nine dead hollow bodies. Ruthless, wasteful, and Vex keeps doing it anyway.
- Future era still shows medieval-tech trappings (banners, cages, torches) mixed with relic-technology (the ring, glass discs, syringes). The past world was roughly equivalent. No modern electronics mentioned.

- Vulnerability: the Court can *feel* a Star awake "under the ring" and comes to claim it within a week. Host-senses.
- The Court runs the city on the Engine but starves the grey-tunicked poor; the scaffold executes "rebels" (those who kept old songs, remembered too far back, asked why the Engine feeds and the city starves). Justice = nooses by threes at dawn.
- The Court's true prize is not the Star's soul: it needs what the King *remembers* — "one name, one map, one night."
- **The Warden's "bread days"** (locked ch9) — the Court's big-file signal: when the Warden is about to move a major file, he orders a bread day for the clerks. Sean has learned to read it.
- **The Ninth Door** (locked ch9, updated ch16) — an Engine room under the mountain where torn-out Stars are "filed"; sealed nine times; a clerk superstition that one more name seals everyone's fate. Contains NINE GLASS CASES holding Vex's RECENT failed extractions (weeks/months ago), NOT the King's original followers from 100 years ago. The King watched Vex's recent extractions — he was sealed in the Door but aware. When a case shatters, the soul breaks free and recognizes people from its recent life (e.g., Marek recognized Elara because he was her guard/brother-in-arms before Vex took him).
- "Everything you love is filed for later" — the Court's ledger philosophy; a thematic motif. Sean's green-field memory is the counterweight.
- **The Warden** — **CASSIAN VARN**. A Court office tied to the ledger/Engine paperwork; has been expecting Sean "for a hundred years." Commands the GREY HAND (the order's oldest enforcement branch; "a hundred ledgers" vs the Keeper's one star). Nature: cold, patient, archival; soft power over direct violence. History with the King [OPEN].
- **The Court's interior / Varn's study** (locked ch8): a bureaucratic fortress of clerks, corridors, and ledgers inside the black walls. Varn keeps no personal guards in his study — a single low chair opposite his desk (height = posture = power), one window, ink warm. He "keeps copies" — the archive holds pages from before the fire, including a page inked with a locust (Sean's childhood) that he lets Sean see as a threat/offer: the old world's records are cover-to-cover in his stacks.
- **The Keeper / the ring's order** — a branch of the same order. The Keeper holds "one star" vs the Court's "hundred ledgers"; a cold war over whose claim the Star falls under. Grief and a private 20-year promise drive her.
- **House Vance (fallen, locked ch7)** — a once-great noble house tied to the old rebellion; "the first to burn with the King." They defied the Night Court alongside him and burned the same night — 112 years ago. The ring's Keeper, Elara, is its last survivor-bearing name. The House seal/ring is a powerful token.
- **The ledger's weapon** (locked ch7): extraction has NEVER preserved a Star's memory — 9 rotted hosts prove it. Therefore the ONLY reliable way the Court gets the King's "one name, one map, one night" is a WILLING host. Sean is the only living key; his life = the map. This is his commercial-strength leverage over the Warden.
